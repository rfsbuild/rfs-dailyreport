Systems & tools

One payment arriving as several bank lines can hide from the cash forecast — the same blind spot as last night's, running the other way
The forecast clears an expected payment by finding a bank line of the same amount. Yesterday two invoices arrived as ONE payment, so neither could be found and the forecast counted $14,275.66 that was already in the account. Today the reverse happened: one payroll-tax payment left as THREE separate bank lines of $1,950.10, $498.76 and $86.45, so again there was no matching line, and the forecast kept expecting $2,530.09 that had already gone. One error made the balance look better than it was and the other made it look worse, which is the useful part — it is the same assumption failing, that one payment always equals one bank line. The fix is to let a group of payments settle against a single line and a single payment against a group of lines, and it is the same change that would let an incoming payment be split across two invoices when it is recorded.
Two charges for the same amount on the same day are not automatically a duplicate
Two card charges of exactly $370.81 appeared on the same day, which normally reads as the same purchase recorded twice. Deleting one would have removed a real payment. What settled it was the statement itself: when this bank renames a charge as it finalises, the old wording DISAPPEARS and is replaced, and there was an example of exactly that on the same statement. Both $370.81 lines were still there side by side. On top of that, the balance only adds up if both are counted. The rule being followed is that a charge is only ever treated as a duplicate on positive evidence that it is one, never on how similar it looks, because the cost of wrongly deleting a real payment is much higher than the cost of checking.
