Systems & tools

The financial files now back themselves up on their own
The balances, the transaction records, the payroll history and the running tracker used to exist in one place only, so a bad write or a mistyped edit had nothing behind it. They now copy themselves into a separate local history on a repeating timer, and every copy is dated, so any one of them can be brought back as it stood at any point. The history is deliberately kept on this machine and nowhere else — it refuses to copy itself anywhere off the computer, because these files carry client names, pay and bank detail. It was also tested against the one failure that matters: a copy taken while a file is half-written is rejected rather than saved, so a broken snapshot can never quietly replace a good one.
The list of everything left behind is now a page you can work through, and it answers back
Every open item found across months of notes was re-checked against the system that actually knows the answer — the payroll records, the ledger, the live balances, the app itself — rather than against the notes, which contradict each other. That produced 214 distinct items, of which 91 genuinely need you; the rest are mine. They are ordered most important first, and where two matter equally the quicker one comes first, each with an estimate of how much of your time it costs. Every item now opens with a single plain sentence saying what was found and what it means, with the full trail of what was checked folded away behind it — nothing removed, just out of the way. There are six ways to answer each one, including handing it to a named person, marking it as waiting on someone with a date to chase them, and asking for it to be explained before you decide. Answers save as you go and can be undone.
