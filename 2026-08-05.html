Systems & tools

A bundled deposit can hide from the cash forecast, and that has been found and fixed
Two invoices worth $14,275.66 arrived as one Buildertrend payment instead of two separate deposits. The forecast clears expected money by matching it to a bank line of the same amount, so with no $10,233.41 line and no $4,042.25 line to find, it left both still projected on top of money already in the account. Corrected, and the projection is now $14,275.66 lower — which is the true figure, not a worse one. Every future bundled payout would do the same thing, so the next step is teaching the match to clear a group of invoices against a single payment.
An independent second pass caught 12 charges that were already in QuickBooks
The first version of the entry list would have created 12 duplicate entries worth $845.78, and it labelled some of the least reliable rows as safe to enter as they were. A separate adversarial check, rebuilt from the raw exports rather than from the first result, found all of it before any of it reached the books — along with two refunds that had never been recorded anywhere. The list went from 95 rows to 83 and now lands on the corrected figure exactly. Checking our own output as hard as we check the bank's is what makes the entry work safe to hand to anyone.
The whole QuickBooks review backlog has been measured against our own records for the first time
1,656 items were sitting in the bank feed waiting to be reconciled, the oldest dating back to January. All of them have now been checked against our records: 1,625 have a match, and the rest are listed one by one instead of left as a bulk number. The valuable part is what it prevents — 644 of those items look like payments already recorded, so clearing the queue the quick way risks recording over a million dollars of cost a second time.
Job costing stops at the card, and now we can say so precisely
Bills carry a project on 62% of their lines, so subcontractors and materials are already costed to the right job. Card charges carry a project on none of theirs. That single gap is the reason card spend cannot be traced to a job today, and it is the same channel the bank checks were thinnest on — two systems, one blind spot. The 83 charges being entered now are the first batch to get a project and a class as they go in.
A finished correction was still labelled unfinished, and doing it twice would have cost real money
Two corrections were made at midday, and the notes describing them still read as though they were waiting. Picked up later in the day, that would have applied both a second time — taking another $14,275.66 of real, banked money out of the projection and deleting a legitimate ledger row. It was settled by opening the backup files the corrections themselves had written, rather than trusting the note. The rule now is that the note announcing a change is updated the moment the change lands, because a status line goes out of date exactly the way a number does.
