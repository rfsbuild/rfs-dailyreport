Systems & tools

A report can go stale without a single number changing
The written notes under the cash plan were typed in by hand. When the plan was rebuilt they stayed as they were, so the figures moved and the sentences describing them did not — and every automated check stayed green, because they test that the page is put together correctly, not whether a sentence still matches the number above it. The notes are now read off the plan itself, so they cannot drift apart again.
An alert that fires every day stops being an alert
The crew's morning email failed seventeen days running, and raised a warning every one of those days. The warning worked perfectly and nobody acted, because nothing distinguishes a problem's first day from its seventeenth. Something that keeps coming back needs to get louder, and today it does not.
The board now records who actually did something
Rafael closing an item used to be indistinguishable from her closing it. It now carries the real name. That matters more than it sounds: the whole point of her being able to hand work over is being able to see afterwards who it went to.
